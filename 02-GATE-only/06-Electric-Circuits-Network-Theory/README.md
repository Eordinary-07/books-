## ✅ Free & legal substitutes for this subject

- ***Lessons In Electric Circuits* Vol 1 (DC) and Vol 2 (AC) — IN THIS REPO ✅** — 1,131 pages combined, fully worked, no calculus prerequisite. Vol 1: Ohm's law, series/parallel, network theorems, DC metering. Vol 2: AC waveforms, reactance, impedance, resonance, filters, transformers, polyphase. This is your free Alexander & Sadiku substitute for theory.
  `00-Common-Library/Lessons-in-Electric-Circuits/`
- **James M. Fiore — *DC Electrical Circuit Analysis* and *AC Electrical Circuit Analysis*** — Two free, properly-typeset open textbooks (CC BY-SA) from Milne Publishing, with worked examples and end-of-chapter problems — closer in structure to a standard engineering circuit text.
  <https://milnepublishing.geneseo.edu/concise-introduction-to-electric-circuits/>
- **MIT OCW 6.002 — Circuits and Electronics (Anant Agarwal)** — The famous free MIT circuits course. Strong on transient analysis, op-amps and frequency response.
  <https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/>
- **Don Johnson — *Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering I*** — Free textbook (CC BY) covering circuit theory, signals, and systems in one volume.
  <https://openstax.org/details/books/fundamentals-electrical-engineering-i>
- **NPTEL — Basic Electrical Circuits / Network Theory** — Indian-syllabus-aligned notes; useful for portal/network-theorem conventions used in GATE.
  <https://archive.nptel.ac.in/courses/108/>

## 📅 Suggested study order

1. One theory book only — do **not** read two in parallel.
2. Free NPTEL/MIT lectures for anything that won't click.
3. Practice book for numericals.
4. PYQs, timed. This is where the marks actually come from.
