## ✅ Free & legal substitutes for this subject

- **Think DSP — Allen B. Downey (IN THIS REPO ✅ — PDF + EPUB)** — Free (CC BY-NC). A genuinely readable, code-first introduction to signals: harmonics, spectra, noise, filtering, sampling, convolution, DFT. The fastest way to build intuition before touching Oppenheim.
  `free-books/thinkdsp.pdf`
- **MIT OCW 6.003 — Signals and Systems (Oppenheim himself)** — The lectures for the actual 'bible' textbook, by its author. Free video + notes. If you only use one free resource for this subject, use this.
  <https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-003-signals-and-systems-fall-2011/>
- **Rich Baraniuk — *Signals and Systems* (Rice University)** — Full free textbook built specifically as an open alternative to Oppenheim (CC BY). Covers LTI systems, Fourier, Laplace, z-transform.
  <https://openstax.org/details/books/signals-and-systems>
- **Steven W. Smith — *The Scientist and Engineer's Guide to DSP*** — Free complete book online. Superb plain-English explanation of sampling, aliasing, convolution and the DFT — the areas GATE loves to test.
  <https://www.dspguide.com/>

## 📅 Suggested study order

1. One theory book only — do **not** read two in parallel.
2. Free NPTEL/MIT lectures for anything that won't click.
3. Practice book for numericals.
4. PYQs, timed. This is where the marks actually come from.
