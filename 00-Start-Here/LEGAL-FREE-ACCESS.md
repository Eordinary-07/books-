# Legal free access — the real map

You asked about PDF Coffee, Internet Archive and "other websites." Those aren't the same kind of thing, and the difference matters a lot for you. This document tells you **exactly where you can legally read each book on your list, for free**, right now.

---

## First, the three categories — because they're not interchangeable

| Type | Examples | What it is | Can I use it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🟢 **Open access / public domain** | Project Gutenberg, OpenStax, NPTEL, MIT OCW, DOAB | Author or publisher released it free, or copyright expired | ✅ Free download, fully legal |
| 🔵 **Library lending** | **Internet Archive / Open Library**, NDLI, DELNET | A real library lending you a scanned copy, one borrower at a time | ✅ Free, legal, needs an account |
| 🔴 **Piracy sites** | PDF Coffee, PDF Drive, LibGen, Sci-Hub | Someone uploaded a scan of a commercial book without permission | ❌ Not doing this |

**Internet Archive is a library, not a piracy site** — that's the important distinction. It has 1.5M+ digitised books and lends them exactly like a physical library: one copy, one borrower, time-limited. That's *Controlled Digital Lending*, and it's how you can legitimately read Kothari & Nagrath or Hayt for free.

**PDF Coffee / PDF Drive / LibGen**: these host scans of in-copyright textbooks that were uploaded without permission. I'm not going to fetch from them, and I'd steer you away anyway — those files are a standard malware vector (embedded scripts, `.pdf.exe` droppers), and they're frequently truncated or the wrong edition. You'd risk your laptop to save ₹400.

So I researched availability via web search (results below, with item IDs you can click) but **could not pull the files from here**. You'll do the borrowing on your own machine.

## 🔵 Internet Archive — how it actually works

**Sign up free** at [archive.org](https://archive.org) or [openlibrary.org](https://openlibrary.org) — just an email. Then, on any book page, look at the availability label:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Borrow** (14 days / 1 hour) | ✅ Free legal loan. Read in the browser, or download an encrypted PDF (Adobe Digital Editions) on 14-day loans. |
| **Read** | ✅ Public domain — read *and* download freely, no loan needed. |
| **Preview Only** | ⚠️ Searchable/flippable only — you can't read the whole thing. Not useful for study. |
| **Locate** | ❌ Not available digitally. |

**The one-hour loan problem:** if the Archive holds only one copy, you get a 1-hour loan and *cannot* download for offline reading. If it holds multiple copies, you can take a 14-day loan and download. When a 14-day copy is out, join the waitlist.

## 🇮🇳 NDLI — the resource you're probably underusing

**National Digital Library of India** — [ndl.iitkgp.ac.in](https://ndl.iitkgp.ac.in) · [ndl.gov.in](https://ndl.gov.in)

Built by **IIT Kharagpur**, funded by the Ministry of Education. **Completely free for Indian students.**

- 1 crore+ items: textbooks, papers, video lectures, previous year papers, simulations
- **Full-text access to nationally-licensed content** (South Asia Archive, World eBook Library) after free registration
- Interface in 10 Indian languages
- Android + iOS apps — works well on a phone
- If you have an `ac.in` / `ernet` / `gov.in` / `nic.in` email, you can self-register instantly

**This is your best bet for the Indian-published books that Internet Archive doesn't hold** — Bimbhra (Khanna), Sawhney (Dhanpat Rai), the Kanodia GATE books, Rajput, J.B. Gupta. Indian publishers license content to NDLI specifically for students like you.

**Also check:**
- **DELNET / N-LIST** — free remote e-book + e-journal access if your college subscribes. Ask your librarian; most RTMNU-affiliated colleges are members. Worth 10 minutes of asking.
- **Your college library** — for Kundur, Krause, Fitzgerald, Kreyszig, Sadiku. These are expensive reference books you'll consult a handful of times. This is what a library is for.

## 🧮 Scilab / FOSSEE Textbook Companions (IIT Bombay)

A genuinely useful and legitimate Indian resource: **FOSSEE** at IIT Bombay builds *Textbook Companion* materials — free, openly-distributed code and notes that follow specific textbooks chapter by chapter.

The search turned up a companion for **Langsdorf's *Theory of Alternating Current Machinery*** (a classic machines text), and others including Kothari & Nagrath. Free and legal.

- **FOSSEE:** [fossee.in](https://fossee.in)
- **Scilab Textbook Companions:** [scilab.in/textbook-companion](https://scilab.in/textbook-companion)

Useful when a book's examples won't click on paper — you get runnable simulations of them.

## 🔍 Check any book yourself in 30 seconds

Availability changes constantly, and I can't query archive.org live from this sandbox. Here's the method:

1. Go to **[openlibrary.org/search](https://openlibrary.org/search)**
2. Search the title. **Open the specific edition** — availability is per-edition, not per-title.
3. Read the label: **Borrow** ✅ / **Read** ✅ / **Preview Only** ⚠️ / **Locate** ❌
4. If the newest edition is Preview Only, **scroll down to the older editions** — as with Hayt, those are often the borrowable ones.

> **The older-edition trick is the single most useful thing in this document.** For engineering fundamentals, a 3rd edition from 1974 teaches you electrostatics exactly as well as an 8th edition from 2018 — and one is a free legal loan while the other isn't. Your own instinct ("if not latest edition then you can go for older edition") was right; it just has to be a *legally available* older edition.
